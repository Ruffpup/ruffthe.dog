One of the things they don’t tell you about a long-distance relationship is how it never gets easier saying goodbye for now.

Over the last two weeks, I was lucky enough to have Kuma over to the US to get to see a taste of what my world looks like. For almost a year now, he’s let me into his world. Staying in his home, introducing me to his friends, and attending events that are important to him. I had almost gotten used to that rhythm, so it was really special when he opted to cross the pond to see me this time.

We had made plans in February for him to come over and support me during my run for Georgia Pup 2026. It was a quick trip over for me to fetch him, have a day in Atlanta to taste some local cuisine, then off to Augusta the following morning.

Kuma was there for me every step of the way during the contest, cheering me on despite his own thoughts on titles and contests. He got to meet people near and dear to me in my community and see what my life is like on this side of an ocean. I flew back with him today, and the feeling of heartbreak at having to part ways again hit hard.

I’m trying my best to remember all of the really amazing memories from the trip. Having him there at the contest, seeing the proud smile when I was given the “Heart of the Class” award. We had an amazing day at the Georgia Aquarium seeing the whale shark and enjoying the shows. Even our Saturday “redneck” outing to try Waffle House and then go shooting at the gun range with Dres, Avi, and Nitro was such a good time.

But sure enough, Sunday morning came, and that feeling of loneliness started to set in. I keep hoping the longer this relationship continues, the easier it gets, but even in talking with others in long-distance relationships, that tugging at your heart never truly goes away.

I know I talk a lot about Alpha, but he’s awakened something in me that I was lacking for a long time: confidence. Confidence in myself and who I am, both as a person and a pup.

I find a lot of comfort in being with him, having him call me his beautiful puppy, and letting me use my name, “Ruff,” when people ask for a name for an order or dinner reservation. He’s helped me to see all of the good things that I do to help those around me and to be proud of the person that I hid for so long.

So yeah, it is hard to say goodbye, even if just for now, because he’s become such an integral part of my life. Sure, we talk for hours via Telegram, and we both really enjoy the time we do have together. He even mentioned how he’d like to try more calls with one another because he does feel a sense of calm around me.

But while the pain of missing him doesn’t seem to get any better, I can definitely feel the bond solidifying between us. It’s taken me a bit to realize that, no matter what, he and I are inextricably linked.

I find comfort in that, especially when I am a bit teary-eyed on the bus ride to the airport and during the 8.5-hour flight back home. I remind myself it’s OK to have those emotions and that I shouldn’t tuck them away.

But one thing is for certain: there will be many more visits to the UK, and soon he will have access to my flight benefits to come back to the States to visit me more. I just need to trust that the bond between us is strong and that, despite being an ocean apart, he will be my Alpha and continue to support me and be proud of all the things that I can accomplish.
