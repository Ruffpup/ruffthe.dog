So last night at pup night the “dark room” was somehow bright enough that everybody got shy. Normally, one of the perks of pup night is having a dark play space for curious pups to sniff, lick and bury bones. Usually, the dark does some of the flirting for you. Last night, we could all see each other thinking about it.

The bar keeps the back room lit now, so the “dark room” isn’t all that dark anymore. There’s something hot about that red glow. It reminds me of a few bathhouses I’ve been to. Still, even after a little social lubrication, everybody seemed shy about getting things going.

I figured I was in for a normal night with friends. Playing wasn’t on my mind when I got there. Then I said hi to someone, we hit it off, and suddenly the red room sounded like a pretty good idea.

Somehow, I got everyone playing anyway.

I kept tuning in and out of what was happening around me. At one point I felt a few hands rubbing my back, and when I looked over, I recognized some faces from the group I usually just hang out with. I’d thought it might be fun to play with some of them before. I figured they weren’t interested, or I was too shy to ask. Turns out I was one of the shy pups in the room, too.

I was having too much fun to pay much attention to the rest of the room. Then I came up from a little butt sniffing and realized we had a small audience. That was a surprise.

The little group in the corner had been trying to be coy about it. Then they moved out into the open, and pups walking by saw what was going on and joined in.

Later, when we were saying our goodbyes, a couple of friends told me how nice the guy I’d been hanging out with was. They asked how long we’d known each other. I looked at my watch.

“About three hours.”

I’ve seen that same slow warm-up at pup moshes at Dogs Days in Augusta and at International Mr. Leather, in the massive dungeon at Thunder in the Mountains back when it was held in Denver, and at Collared in London. And there it was again, under the red lights at the Heretic in Atlanta.

Walking into an event alone for the first time can be overwhelming. The room is unfamiliar, everyone seems to know each other, and even figuring out where to stand can feel scary. When you’re a regular, saying hi and introducing someone to a couple of friends can take less than a minute. For the person who just walked in, that minute can change how they feel about the whole place. It might even be the reason they come back.

Sometimes it’s a little puppy wave. Sometimes it’s a compliment on someone’s gear or outfit. Sometimes you just catch someone’s eye under the red lights. I’ve lost count of how many times Alpha or I have waved a new pup over and introduced them to the group. I love getting to share the things that made me want to keep coming back.

At the Eagle, I’d seen a pup in his hood wandering slowly through the bar, like he was looking for a place to land. A little later, I spotted him standing by himself in the middle of the patio. He looked my way, so I gave him a little puppy wave.

I could see his energy change. He came right over and said hi. We did introductions, and before long we were inviting him to come to the Ren Faire with us the next day. It all started with a wave from across the patio.

Once we got talking, he was joking with us and giving hugs. At one point he even took off his pup hood to chat. I’d seen him wandering the bar by himself earlier, and now here he was, settling right in with a group of pups he’d only just met.

I still remember being the shy pup at International Mr. Leather. I didn’t know anyone, and I had no idea what to expect. One of the guys setting up smiled and waved me over. I wonder if he even remembers doing it. I do. That little wave helped set me on the path to becoming the pup I am now.

You can come to an event because you already know you want to be part of this community and still feel completely alone once you get there. Then somebody says hi and introduces you to two or three other pups, and you can see the moment your shoulders drop. You get to be the puppy you came there to be. I love watching somebody light up when they finally get to enjoy the event the way I do now.

I don’t play at events all that often, but when I do, I remember why I love this community and why I love puppy play. Pet play can be sexual, silly, or both. For me, it’s about giving yourself permission to have fun.

At the Georgia Renaissance Festival, we were waiting for a few friends to get back from the shops so we could take a Georgia Kennel Club group photo. A bard was playing a song about mushrooms that had no business being that catchy. I didn’t even realize I was bopping along until Dante caught me on video.

Sometimes I hold back because I worry about how I’ll look or what people will think. But when I finally saw the video, I just smiled. I’d been so caught up bopping along to this ridiculous mushroom song that I’d slipped into pup headspace without even realizing it. What was I so worried about looking like, anyway? A corgi having a good time?

Sometimes it’s getting a little feral under a red light and looking up to find that the pups who were too shy to play five minutes ago are joining in.

And sure, some of us with social anxiety still have to work up to that first hello. But a hello can turn into a few introductions, a drink with new friends, and maybe, just maybe, a room full of pups playing under a red light because one chaos corgi decided to go first.
